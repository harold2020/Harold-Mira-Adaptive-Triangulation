    How this simulation tests your idea +

    What the car receives

    Up to 1,000 ray-cast wall distances across a 270° field of view, plus its own speed and steering. Walls block the rays. No centerline or future track layout is passed to the controller.

    How it steers

    Nearby points influence a wider range of directions. The controller finds an opening, compares short steering arcs, and limits speed for visible clearance, braking strength, and turning ability.

    Handling challenge

    In reduced-grip mode, actual braking and lateral grip are 65% of the controller’s initial nominal estimate. This deliberate mismatch tests recovery. Failures are produced by the physics, not scripted. Nominal-grip mode removes that mismatch.

    What a retry changes

    A contact crash or four-second stop restores an earlier snapshot. Each failure multiplies the pace by 0.83 (floor 0.28), adds 0.10 m clearance (cap 0.65 m), and adds preview. Repeated failures at one location rewind farther. These are explicit rules, not a trained neural model.

    What this does not establish

    This is a simplified 2D physics test with perfect range measurements and static walls. Screenshot recognition and real sensors are outside this toy simulation. A green arc is an intended path, not a safety guarantee.
